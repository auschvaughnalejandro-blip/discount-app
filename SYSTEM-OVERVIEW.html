System overview · Internal

The Privilege Guest Program, as software

An invitation-only hotel benefits programme currently run on printed letters and plastic cards, rebuilt as a guest app and an administrator panel — with the confidentiality of the membership list treated as the primary design constraint rather than a later addition.

Property Steigenberger Doha Definition Version 3 Stage Pre-production Compiled 10 August 2026

1 The programme, and the problem

The Privilege Guest Program already exists. What does not exist is any digital trace of it.

Membership is extended personally by the Cluster General Manager. A member receives three things: an invitation letter, a numbered plastic card printed with their name, and a printed sheet of benefits. The reference card in the programme materials is number three — this is a small, deliberately exclusive list.

The benefits are a fixed discount schedule, not a points currency:

The benefit schedule as it stands on paper
CategoryBenefitConstraints
Food & Beverage 25% off · 50% for children 6–12 · free for children 0–6 Maximum 6 people
Rooms & Suites 30% off published bar rates Subject to availability
Spa 40% off treatments · 25% off retail Maximum 2 people
Meetings & Events 25% off events · 20% off outside catering Minimum 20 people
Lifestyle 30% off memberships · 25% off pool day pass · free valet & wifi —

Two problems, one on each side of the desk

For the member

The benefits live on a sheet of paper that will be lost. There is no way to check an entitlement while standing in a restaurant, and every reservation means finding the right phone number on a printed table.

For the hotel

There is no data at all. Nobody can say how many members used the spa discount last month, which benefit is most popular, whether the programme earns its cost, or which members have never used anything. The programme runs blind.

The printed benefits sheet becomes an app, and the administrator panel gives the hotel one place to manage membership, decide requests, and record each use.

What this deliberately is not

The first version of the specification assumed a multi-brand coalition with a points currency. The client narrowed it twice. The reductions are recorded so they read as decisions rather than omissions:

  • No points, ledger or balances. Nothing is earned; benefits are granted on joining.
  • No tiers. One membership level, and every member sees the same schedule.
  • No POS integration. The discount is applied on the hotel's own till, exactly as it is today. The system records that it happened; it never touches money.
  • No payment processing, no public signup, no multi-property support.
  • No staff verification app. Version 3 removed the counter-facing surface entirely — see §2.

2 What was built

Two applications, one shared backend. There is no third surface and no second account type.

The member guest app

Phone-first, reachable from anywhere. Seven screens:

  • Activate — enter the invitation code from the letter
  • Sign in / Verify — phone number, then a one-time passcode
  • Offers — the full benefit list, nothing hidden behind a tap
  • Offer detail — terms, party-size limits, tap-to-call reservations, and the button that asks to use it
  • Profile — contact details, communication consent, redemption history
  • Card — the digital equivalent of the plastic card: name and membership number

The administrator panel

Desktop, and reachable only from the hotel network. Six sections:

  • Members — create, search, suspend, reinstate, reissue an invitation code
  • Requests — the queue of members asking to use a benefit; approve or decline
  • Redemptions — record a use, reverse a mistake, review the full log
  • Benefits — edit percentages, terms, caps and reservation numbers; publish and unpublish
  • Reports — usage by month, benefit and outlet; dormant and unclaimed members; CSV export
  • Administrators — create accounts, suspend them, reset a lost second factor

The digital card carries no QR code

Earlier drafts specified a scannable, rotating credential and a counter-staff verification page to scan it. Version 3 removed both. The card now shows a name and a membership number, and entitlement is established by an administrator deciding a request — not by a member presenting a code that a machine trusts.

Why this matters commercially

A 40% spa discount is worth real money. A self-declared or screenshot-forwardable credential would be worth stealing. Routing every use through a named person who approves it means the discount is never granted by software alone.

One account type on the hotel side

Manager, support and outlet-staff roles were specified in version 2 and removed in version 3. Only named Administrator accounts can sign in. The old role values survive in the database purely so that historical records keep naming whoever performed an action; they are forced suspended, hold no permissions, and cannot authenticate.

4 The workflows

Six flows carry the whole product. Everything else is a list or a form.

4.1 Bringing a member onto the app

The programme is invitation-only, so onboarding runs in the opposite direction from a normal app — the hotel creates the account, and the member claims it.

  1. Administrator

    Create the member record

    Name, and optionally the phone number and email the hotel already holds. The system assigns the next membership number in sequence — PG-0004, and so on.

  2. System

    Generate a single-use invitation code

    160 bits of randomness, formatted in groups for a printed letter. It is shown to the administrator exactly once and stored only as a hash — it cannot be produced again, only reissued.

  3. Hotel

    Deliver the invitation

    Printed on the letter alongside the physical card, or sent directly. The code expires after 30 days by default.

  4. Member

    Activate

    Enter the code and a phone number. Where the hotel already recorded a number, the one supplied must match it. Consent is captured per channel, unticked by default.

  5. System

    Consume the code and open the session

    The code is marked used inside the same database transaction that activates the membership, so two simultaneous attempts cannot both succeed. The card appears.

Why single-use matters here

The letter and the card both carry the membership number. A code that could be reused would let anyone holding a discarded letter join the programme. Reissuing a code invalidates the previous one, so a stray copy stops working.

4.2 Signing in — the two paths

A member

  1. Enters their phone number
  2. Receives a six-digit passcode, valid five minutes
  3. Enters it — five wrong attempts and the code is dead

No password ever exists for a member, so none can be reused from another breach. The response is identical whether or not the number belongs to a member.

An administrator

  1. Email and password (Argon2id, screened against breach lists)
  2. A short-lived challenge is issued — not a session
  3. A six-digit code from their authenticator app

A correct password alone yields nothing. First sign-in forces enrolment; ten single-use recovery codes are shown once, at that moment, and never again.

4.3 The main loop — request, decide, record

This is the flow the product exists for, and the one place where the design is deliberately unusual: a member never grants themselves anything.

Four properties of this loop are enforced rather than assumed:

  • One open request per benefit. A member tapping twice does not put two identical rows in the queue for someone to approve twice.
  • One decision. Two administrators working the queue simultaneously cannot both decide the same row; the second is told it has already been dealt with.
  • One approval, spent once. The database holds a unique constraint linking an approval to at most one redemption — enforced by the schema, not by a handler remembering to check.
  • Party-size limits are checked at recording. A spa redemption for three guests is refused, because the cap is two.

4.4 Recording a use, and correcting one

When a redemption is recorded, the discount percentage in force at that moment is copied onto the row. It is never read back from the benefit later.

Changing the dining discount from 25% to 20% changes what members are offered tomorrow. It does not rewrite what a member was given last month, and it does not restate a report that has already been sent.

Redemptions cannot be edited or deleted — not by policy, but because the database account the application uses has had UPDATE and DELETE revoked on that table. A correction is a new reversing row carrying a negated amount and pointing at the original, attributed to whoever authorised it. Totals therefore net correctly without any query needing to know about reversals.

Every recording carries a client-supplied idempotency key, so a retried submission on a bad connection returns the original record rather than creating a second one.

4.5 Changing a benefit without a deployment

The programme materials state that benefits are subject to change without notice. That made editability the single most important configuration requirement: every value a member sees is a database row, never a constant in code.

The test of success

Changing the F&B discount from 25% to 20% is a form field, not a code change. Each edit increments a version number and records who made it — "who changed the spa discount, and when" is answerable.

4.6 Reporting and export

The panel answers usage by month, by benefit and by outlet; average party size; estimated discount value given; and the two lists the hotel cannot currently produce at all — members who have never redeemed anything, and members issued a card who never activated the app.

Export produces a CSV a person can open, containing membership numbers rather than names. It is a separate permission, limited to five per administrator per day, and every export is individually recorded.

A note in the schema worth quoting

"There are no points, tiers, balances or earned currency in this programme. If a balance column ever appears here, it is a misreading of the specification."

Money is stored as integer minor units — fils — and never as a floating point number. Percentages are exact decimals. The one place discount value is computed twice (once in SQL for the dashboard, once per row for the member's own history) is held to the same arithmetic by a test.

6 Security

The membership list is the asset. Everything in this section follows from that, not from a generic checklist.

The reference card in the programme materials is issued to a member of the Qatari ruling family. A membership list of this kind is a record of named, prominent individuals and their movements — when they dined, when they visited the spa, how often they stay.

That is not ordinary customer data. A leak would be a serious matter for the hotel independent of any regulatory penalty — which is why confidentiality controls here go beyond what a loyalty app would normally carry.

6.1 Authorisation — deny by default, enforced at startup

Every route must declare which permission it requires. A route that declares nothing does not fail at runtime with a 403 — it prevents the server from starting at all.

Why this is the strongest control in the system

A wrapper you have to remember to call is exactly the control that fails under deadline. Here, forgetting to declare a permission is not a hole discovered in production; it is a server that will not boot. Public endpoints exist, but they must say 'public' explicitly — the opt-out is greppable.

Permissions are listed exhaustively per role rather than by wildcard, so adding a new permission to the catalogue never silently grants it to anyone.

6.2 Scoping — the query, not the result

Records are filtered in the database WHERE clause, never fetched and then checked. Loading a record first and testing it afterwards leaks its existence through timing and error shape even when the response is eventually a 404.

The scope combinator uses AND rather than an object spread — a subtle but real distinction, because a spread would let a member's own identifier silently overwrite the one they asked about, returning their own record for any ID they tried. A dedicated test file (fetch-then-check.test.ts) exists to hold this line.

Replay protection on the second factor

A TOTP code stays valid for roughly ninety seconds. The system records the time period of the last code each account used and refuses anything at or before it — so a code read over a shoulder cannot be used a second time inside its own window. Member passcodes are single-use for the same reason; a staff second factor should not be weaker.

6.4 Sessions and tokens

  • Two audiences. A token minted for the member app is rejected by an administrator endpoint at the audience check, before it is inspected at all.
  • Algorithm pinned. Verification is called with a fixed algorithm allowlist, so alg: none and algorithm-confusion attacks fail before the signature is examined.
  • Short lifetimes. Ten minutes for the panel, thirty for the member app.
  • Instant revocation. Every account carries a token version, checked on every request. Suspending a member or an administrator invalidates all their outstanding tokens immediately — they do not stay valid until expiry.
  • Rotation with reuse detection. Refresh tokens are single-use and grouped into families. Presenting a spent one revokes the whole family and forces re-authentication, because a legitimate client never replays one.
  • No token in browser storage. The refresh token lives in a cookie marked httpOnly, Secure and SameSite=Strict, scoped to the single endpoint that reads it. A test strips comments from every client source file and asserts no localStorage, sessionStorage or document.cookie use.

6.5 Confidentiality of the membership

Names never reach the logs

Two mechanisms, because either alone leaves a gap: the logger redacts known paths, and a serialiser walks whatever it is handed and removes anything named like personal data at any depth. That second layer is what makes an accidental "log the whole member object" call safe — enforced by a redaction layer rather than by convention.

Reports cannot be used to identify a single person

With a membership of dozens, a report filtered to one benefit, one outlet and one week may describe exactly one individual. Any figure backed by fewer than five distinct members returns "insufficient data" instead of a number — and the cohort size itself is withheld too, since publishing "3 members" is the same disclosure stated outright.

Exports carry numbers, not names

The CSV is the one artefact that leaves the system, and it identifies members by membership number. It is a separate permission, rate-limited to five per administrator per day, and both successful and throttled attempts are recorded.

Health data is excluded by construction

Spa treatment records can imply health information. The redemption table has no column for a treatment or description, and no endpoint accepts one — only that a spa benefit was used.

6.6 Resistance to probing

  • Account enumeration. Requesting a passcode returns the same response whether or not the number belongs to a member — on a membership this exclusive, confirming that a phone number is on the list is itself a disclosure.
  • Timing. A sign-in against a non-existent account still runs a full Argon2 verification against a dummy hash, so it costs the same wall-clock time as a real one.
  • Uniform failures. Unknown, expired, already-used and wrong-phone invitation codes all return one identical message. Which one it was is exactly what someone holding a discarded letter would want to learn.
  • Rate limits on sign-in, passcode request and verification, activation, second-factor verification, and export — applied per IP and per identifier, so rotating IPs does not buy a fresh budget of guesses.
  • Pagination caps so no endpoint can be coerced into returning the whole membership.
  • No client-supplied SQL, ever. Report metrics and dimensions come from a server-side allowlist; an unknown name never reaches a query builder.

6.7 Integrity at the database level

The application connects as a role that is not the schema owner. That role has had UPDATE and DELETE revoked on redemptions, the audit log and consent records, and DELETE revoked on members. Migrations run as the owner over a separate connection, so the revocation cannot be undone by the migration that creates the tables.

Why the role split is the point

An owner cannot be denied access to its own tables. Immutability only means something because the application is not the owner — if both used the same account, the guarantee would be decorative.

6.8 The audit trail

Thirty-eight distinct actions are recorded, including the ones a system like this is usually missing: every member record viewed and by whom, every export, every benefit change, every authorisation denial, every refresh-token replay, and every approval or decline with the deciding administrator named.

Audit metadata carries membership numbers, never names. A failed sign-in against a non-existent account deliberately does not record the email tried — otherwise the attacker's guesses would be written into the trail.

6.9 At the edge

  • Automatic TLS with two-year HSTS, preload-eligible
  • A strict Content-Security-Policy with no unsafe-inline; frame-ancestors 'none', object-src 'none'
  • X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff, Referrer-Policy: no-referrer, camera/microphone/geolocation/payment denied
  • Server version banners removed
  • The database publishes no port — it is reachable only on the internal container network
  • The API container runs read-only, with no-new-privileges
  • Administrator routes are refused from outside the hotel network on the API path, not just the panel's hostname

The functional build is complete: both applications, all six workflows, the reporting and export layer, and the administrator account management added most recently. Twenty of twenty-two test files pass in full.

The 25 failing tests have a single cause

Every failure traces to one recently added guard. Member creation now refuses a record with no email address when passcodes are delivered by email — because such a member could never activate the app, and the failure would otherwise surface days later at the guest's first attempt, with nothing on the administrator's screen having suggested a problem.

The guard is correct and has its own passing test. Two older test files create members without an email address and now receive a 400 where they expect a 201; every assertion after that point in those files cascades.

Finding · low severity, quick fix

The fix is to add an email address to the member-creation helpers in member-lifecycle.test.ts and acceptance-journey.test.ts. No product code is wrong. Until it is done, the suite reports red, which will hide the next real regression.

Two other findings from this review

Finding · would block a production start

docker-compose.prod.yml still requires a VERIFICATION_SESSION_HMAC_SECRET variable. That secret belonged to the verification page, which version 3 removed — the application no longer reads it, and it is not listed in .env.example. Because the compose file marks it mandatory, the production stack will refuse to start until the line is deleted or the variable is set to a throwaway value.

Finding · documentation drift

SECURITY-REVIEW.md has fallen behind the code in three places. It lists secure cookies as deferred (they are implemented), it states that no security headers are configured (the edge configuration sets a full policy), and it refers throughout to three client applications and a verification page that no longer exist. The control summary in §6.10 above reflects the code as it actually stands.

8 What remains before launch

In the order I would take them.

  1. Engineering · small

    Green the test suite and remove the stale production secret

    Both findings in §7. Hours, not days — but the second one is the difference between a deployment that starts and one that does not.

  2. Decision · client

    Settle passcode delivery

    Sign-in codes currently go by email, because no SMS provider was provisioned. The programme's own materials say members are notified by their preferred channel, and a member who gave only a phone number cannot receive a code at all. This needs a provider and a budget line, not more code.

  3. Decision · legal

    Hosting region and a data protection impact assessment

    Qatar's data protection law restricts transferring personal data outside the country, and failing to carry out an assessment carries an administrative fine of up to QAR 1,000,000 on its own — independent of any breach. Both are blocking for launch and neither is an engineering task.

  4. Engineering · medium

    Alerting

    Repeated failed lookups, refresh-token replay, bulk export, out-of-hours administrative access and spikes in authorisation denials are all already recorded as distinct events. Nothing consumes them, which makes the audit trail forensic rather than preventive. A destination needs choosing.

  5. Engineering · medium

    Key management and rotation

    The password pepper and the token signing key are environment variables. Moving them into a managed key service, and adding a key identifier so old tokens still verify during a rollover, retires the last of the deferred credential items.

  6. Engineering · small

    An anonymisation endpoint

    A deletion request must be honoured by irreversibly severing the link to redemption history rather than removing rows, so aggregate reporting survives. The schema already supports this; the endpoint does not exist.

  7. Decision · client

    Approve or drop the Google Sheets mirror

    An optional one-way export to a hotel-facing spreadsheet is built and disabled by default. It carries membership numbers, usage counts and benefit configuration, and deliberately excludes names, phone numbers, emails, request notes, consents and audit records. It is a standing bulk disclosure and should be an explicit decision rather than a default.

Questions still open with the client

  • Should the app cover the priority reservations and member-event invitations promised in the letter but absent from the benefits sheet?
  • Should staff record the bill amount at redemption? It is the only route to knowing what the programme costs — the field exists and is optional today.
  • Does membership expire or renew?
  • Who issues invitations, and is there an approval step?
  • Arabic as well as English? The layout was built with logical properties throughout so right-to-left is a single attribute rather than a second stylesheet, but no translation exists.
  • What are the three numbers you would check every month? The fastest way to confirm the reporting scope is right.
